A. About Me

Amir Farahani

My name is Amirhossein Hassankhani Farahani and I am from Tehran, Iran. I am a third-year Computer Science for Software Development student at York University Markham Campus. I have well developed analytical skills and I enjoy learning new topics in mathematics, philosophy, and computer science. I think my proclivity to actively pursue challenging topics puts me in a unique position to tackle research in computer science.

I grew up in a privileged family and I attended academically oriented private schools. The private schools had a big emphasis on STEM subjects and making the students engage with topics much beyond their grade level. This experience made me see the beauty in learning challenging subjects and equipped me with the knowledge and skills to do so.

I always knew that I wanted to study a mathematically oriented subject in university. When it came down to choosing, I decided to go with computer science since I also took a liking to logic and programming. I got accepted into York University's new Markham campus and enrolled in the CSSD program. I believe the skills and experiences I built earlier in my life helped me maintain a high GPA and be successful in the program.

The most influential teacher I had in my life was a man named Mohammadreza Khatami. He was my middle school science teacher and he sparked my interest in many different scientific topics. This in turn motivated me to research and learn more about those subjects to discuss them with him and ask more questions. The skills I learned from my interactions with him have proven to be the most valuable gifts I have ever received.

"If people never did silly things nothing intelligent would ever get done." : Ludwig Wittgenstein

This quote taught me that it is not unintelligent to try new solutions that seem stupid. No matter how many times your ideas fail to achieve a desirable result, one novel idea will, and the only way to find the successful idea is to experiment. I have selected this artifact since it represents the resilience needed when engaging with demanding topics and challenging problems.

Vision

I envision a future where the systems people depend on to live their daily lives, the ones protecting their personal information, informing their decisions, and deciding what content they are recommended are built with the same transparency as my password manager and the same efficiency as the optimized searching algorithm in my chess engine. Efficient enough to be practical, private enough to be safe, and open enough to be verified rather than simply believed. As a computer scientist and software developer, what drives me isn't just building something novel, it's building something better and more honest. I want my ideals to be the standard at global scale, technology built for everyone with access to the internet, not gatekept behind proprietary licences, but freely auditable by anyone who wants to look at the source code.

This vision serves the common good because privacy, access, and transparency aren't luxuries reserved for the economic elite, rather they're human rights in our digital age. Whether people are members of an underserved community with limited access to technology or part of the affluent silicon valley crowd should not be a deciding factor for access to these rights. A password manager can only be trusted if its source is open for scrutiny, well-optimized software only matters if it reaches beyond the people who have access to high-end hardware. What makes this vision mine is the belief that optimization, privacy, and openness aren't separate goals, rather they are all parts of a greater goal, putting people ahead of profits. I don't just want to build things that didn't exist before, I want to build things that didn't exist before, with privacy, while being open source, for the people who need them most.
