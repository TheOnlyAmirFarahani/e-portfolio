Section D — Reflections

Co-op Research Journal

Weekly reflections from my undergraduate research placement with Prof. Marios Fokaefs, May–August 2026. Learning objectives: get better at asking for and using feedback, learn performance modelling and Palladio, and learn how to research and write abstracts.

Week 1
Retell

Read through the codebases of several existing lab projects and generated sequence diagrams for one of them.

Relate

Learned Docker for containerizing applications into microservices, and AppMap, a tool for capturing and analyzing the runtime behaviour of a JVM application.

Reflect

The workload was light, which made the week easy, but my first diagrams didn't actually use AppMap, so I had to redo them in week two. I learned that I forget tasks if I don't write them down — a small process fix that mattered more than it sounds.

Use of Engineering ToolsInvestigation
Week 2
Retell

Regenerated the sequence diagrams using AppMap for consistency, then generated a second set of diagrams using an LLM.

Relate

Applied my AppMap knowledge in practice, and learned how to generate PlantUML diagrams from an LLM — including how to write prompts that actually produce usable output.

Reflect

My AI-generated diagrams had issues at first. My supervisor's advice was simple — adjust the prompts and experiment — and that worked. I learned that I'm able to take initiative when a first attempt doesn't land.

Use of Engineering ToolsDesign
Week 3
Retell

Generated more diagrams using different techniques and put together a slideshow to present progress to my supervisor.

Relate

Learned how to build a presentation specifically for showing research progress, not just results.

Reflect

My supervisor said the diagrams were in good shape and asked me to analyze where each LLM was right or wrong the following week. I noticed I get a bit nervous while presenting — something worth working on directly, which is part of why I set a written communication SMART goal.

Communication Skills
Week 4
Retell

Analyzed the diagrams from the previous week, updated the slideshow with results, and got more familiar with the project's codebase.

Relate

Learned how to compare LLM-generated diagrams against AppMap output to judge quality, and how to present findings concisely.

Reflect

My supervisor was satisfied with the work, and I think this was the week I actually learned how to take feedback and turn it into better output rather than just noting it and moving on.

Week 5
Retell

Got up to speed on "devbot," a tool built by previous researchers to automate LLM-based diagram generation, and modified its source code to add features my research needed.

Relate

Learned the API needed to connect to LLMs and generate artifacts programmatically, and learned how to fork a project on GitHub and add custom features cleanly.

Reflect

My supervisor confirmed the changes would increase productivity and produce more uniform results for the lab. I learned that I'm able to extend and adapt existing tools, not just use them as-is.

My supervisor was content with the quality of the output. This was the week I recognized my own problem-solving and analytical skills as a real strength, not just something I assumed about myself.

Reflection on Learning Objectives

How well did I meet my objectives?

I think I genuinely learned how to use feedback to improve my work — a skill I can keep sharpening, but one that's already visibly better, since my supervisor was consistently satisfied after I applied his advice. On Palladio, I've got the fundamentals down: I can run simulations and build models, which was the goal, even though the tool has plenty of features beyond what my research needs. Research and abstract writing is the objective I haven't met yet — I understand the basics of collecting and analyzing objective evidence, but I haven't started my abstract, since I'm still only in the first half of my placement.

What I'm carrying forward

The most valuable things I picked up were soft skills, not tools: turning feedback into concrete improvements, presenting progress concisely, and working independently on unfamiliar problems without needing constant direction. Learning to spot my own weak points quickly and adapt is the piece of this placement I expect to matter most in the long run.
