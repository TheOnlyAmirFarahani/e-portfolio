Portfolio: Dreams & Goals

I always knew that I wanted to study a mathematically oriented subject in university. When it came down to choosing, I decided to go with computer science since I also took a liking to logic and programming. I got accepted into York University's new Markham campus and enrolled in the CSSD program. I believe the skills and experiences I built earlier in my life helped me maintain a high GPA and be successful in the program.

I want to be a computer science researcher, enabling me to be independent, earn a high income, and engage intellectually and creatively with my work. This in turn enables me to live a financially stable and secure life outside of my work.

Portfolio: Interests & Skills

Communication Skills

As a 3rd-year Computer Science student, pure technical skills are not enough to succeed. The ability to convey complex and abstract ideas to people with different backgrounds is the gap between talented developers and extraordinary ones. In my co-op research role, I frequently need to document findings and present my work in weekly meetings. I have noticed that my writing, while being sufficient, doesn't really have a great structure and is not successful at summarizing my weekly updates. Similarly, my oral communication can be improved and developed further, improving my career prospects.

Goal 1: Written Communication

By August 31, 2026, I will improve the quality of my technical writing by completing the Microsoft professional writing course on Coursera, applying one lesson at a time to my weekly reports, and achieving a writing score of at least 80% on my next two course assignments that include a written component in the Fall.

  • Specific: Microsoft professional writing course completion.
  • Measurable: One lesson a week.
  • Attainable: Course is online and asynchronous.
  • Relevant: Builds good professional writing fundamentals.
  • Time-bound: Deadline of August 31, 2026, before the academic year.

Goal 2: Oral Communication

By July 15, 2026, I will improve my oral presentation skills. I will do this by asking for feedback at the end of my routine weekly presentation at my co-op placement and implementing the feedback on the subsequent presentations.

  • Specific: 4 weekly presentations at my co-op. I will write down supervisor feedback and apply them for the next presentation.
  • Measurable: 4 presentations delivered, and at least 1 improvement done each time.
  • Attainable: Doing weekly presentations is a part of my job, and my supervisor is open to giving me feedback.
  • Relevant: Directly applies to oral presentation and communications skills.
  • Time-bound: Deadline of July 15, 2026.

Portfolio: Values & Personality

Reflecting on my values assessment made me realize what motivates my career choices. I highly ranked earning a high salary, having opportunities for significant salary growth, job security, engaging in intellectually stimulating and creative work, not experiencing high pressure, helping others, maintaining a healthy work-life balance, and working independently. These results were not surprising at all since these values attracted me to study computer science, since it enables me to earn a high income while being independent and engaging in intellectually stimulating work.

Similarly, my lifestyle values emphasize building a stable and secure life. I value owning a home, living in a safe neighborhood, spending time with family and friends, pursuing creative hobbies, and having opportunities to enjoy arts and entertainment all ranked highly for me. This suggests that I care deeply about my life outside of work.

One important value that is missing from here is my love of learning. Perhaps this can be attributed to doing intellectually stimulating work, but that does not translate into constantly learning new topics and experimenting with them.

Additionally, there are some conflicts in my values such as earning a high-income but having low pressure and being independent but having job security. Despite this, the vision I have for my future encompasses all of these values. I want to be a computer science researcher, enabling me to be independent, earn a high income, and engage intellectually and creatively with my work. This in turn enables me to live a financially stable and secure life outside of my work.
